Crown & Court

A political card game of gold, bluffing, alliances, and succession

Goal: Whoever holds the King’s seat when the final round ends wins the game.

4–6 players · 5 rounds (3 for a quick game)
King deck: 42 cards · Noble deck: 40 cards per Noble

What you need

ItemStandard
Players4–6 (best at 6)
Rounds5 (use 3 for a quick game)
King deck42 cards (one copy in play)
Noble deck40 cards (each Noble has their own shuffled copy)
Gold tokens or paperTrack each player’s wealth
Pen & paper (optional)Track earned gold separately from gifts

Setup

  1. Choose the starting King — pick randomly, or agree on a house rule.
  2. Assign roles — the King uses the King deck; each Noble gets their own shuffled Noble deck.
  3. Starting gold — King: 1,000 · each Noble: 600. All of this starts as earned gold.
  4. Deal hands — each player draws 8 cards.
  5. Turn direction — odd rounds go one way around the table; even rounds reverse.

Gold: earned vs. gifted

This is the most important rule. Only earned gold counts toward becoming King.
  • Earned gold — starting wealth, card effects, and anything not a direct gift from another player during negotiation.
  • Gifted gold — gold another player gave you during the negotiation phase. You can spend it, but it does not help you win the crown.

Gold moved by card effects (theft, taxes, etc.) follows the card — not the gift rules.

Roles & succession

One King’s seat exists at the table. The person there plays from the King deck; everyone else plays from the Noble deck.

Succession checks happen twice each round:

  1. After negotiation
  2. After playing cards

At each check, if any Noble has more earned gold than the current King, the highest-earned Noble (ties broken by Noble play order that round) takes the crown.

When someone becomes King:

  • They swap roles, deck, hand, and discard pile with the former King.
  • Gold stays with the player — your coins follow you even if you change seats.

Some cards can block succession for a check (e.g. Loyal Guard).

Round structure

Negotiation → Succession → Play cards → Succession → Next round

1. Negotiation (4 table passes)

Starting from the King, each player gets 4 full passes around the table. On your turn you may:

  • Propose a trade — offer gold and/or cards; the other player may accept or reject.
  • Propose an alliance — a declared pact (some cards require an active alliance).
  • Make a conditional promise — table talk only; not binding unless a card says otherwise.
  • Threaten or bluff
  • Pass

Gift limits (recommended for balanced play):

LimitAmount
Max gold per single gift120
Max gold you can give per negotiation phase120
Max gold in one trade (each side)120
Max completed trades per player per phase2

At the end of negotiation → succession check.

2. Play cards

Everyone chooses cards face-down, then reveals in order:

  1. King plays 3 cards (in the order they chose).
  2. Each Noble plays 2 cards, in turn order from the King (respecting the round’s direction).

Cards resolve one at a time. Some ask you to pick a target or make a choice.

Redraw after all cards resolve:

  • King draws 3 cards.
  • Each Noble draws 2 cards.

Hands refill up to 8 when possible (shuffle discard into deck when empty).

Then → second succession check → next round.

Protection cards

Protection cards are a gamble:

  • Play them face-down with your other cards.
  • After all cards are revealed, each protection card is checked:
    • Hit — you guessed correctly; you get the benefit.
    • Whiff — your read was wrong; you pay the penalty (usually losing gold).

The King resolves first each round, so King protection against succession is more reliable than Noble protection against attacks that have not happened yet.

Winning

When the last round finishes, whoever sits in the King’s seat wins. There is no shared victory.

Tips for your first game

  1. Track earned gold — jot down each player’s earned total when gifts happen.
  2. Negotiation is not optional — trades and alliances set up betrayal cards.
  3. The King goes first — plan reveals knowing the King’s cards resolve before Nobles’.
  4. Gifts are for influence, not the crown — bribes do not inflate succession score.
  5. Protection is a read — a wrong guess can hurt you.

Short game variant

Play 3 rounds instead of 5; keep all other standard numbers.

Good luck at court. Trust no one — especially the King.
