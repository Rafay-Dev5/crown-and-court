# Crown & Court — Rules

A 4–6 player political card game of gold, bluffing, alliances, and succession.  
**Goal:** Whoever holds the **King’s seat** when the final round ends wins.

**Print / PDF:** open [`rules-print.html`](rules-print.html) in a browser → **Print → Save as PDF** (or use the on-page button).

---

## What you need

| Item | Standard game |
|------|----------------|
| Players | **4–6** (best at 6) |
| Rounds | **5** (use **3** for a quick game) |
| King deck | **42 cards** (shared design; one copy in play) |
| Noble deck | **40 cards** (each Noble has their own shuffled copy) |
| Gold tokens or paper | Track each player’s wealth |
| Pen & paper (optional) | Track **earned gold** separately from gifts |

---

## Setup

1. **Choose the starting King** — pick randomly, or agree on a house rule (youngest player, last winner, etc.).
2. **Assign roles**
   - The starting King uses the **King deck**.
   - Everyone else is a **Noble** and gets their own shuffled **Noble deck**.
3. **Starting gold**
   - **King:** 1,000 gold  
   - **Each Noble:** 600 gold  
   - At the start, all of this counts as **earned gold** (see below).
4. **Deal hands** — each player draws **8** cards.
5. **Turn direction** — odd-numbered rounds go one way around the table; even rounds reverse (see *Round structure*).

---

## Gold: earned vs. gifted

This is the most important rule in the game.

- **Earned gold** — gold you got from starting wealth, card effects, and anything *not* a direct gift from another player during negotiation. **Only earned gold counts toward becoming King.**
- **Gifted gold** — gold another player gave you during the **negotiation phase** (trades, gifts, bribes). You can **spend** gifted gold normally, but it **does not** help you win the crown.

> **Why?** So a player who is out of the running cannot simply dump their treasury on a friend in the last round and decide who wins.

When gold moves by **card effects** (theft, taxes, etc.), it follows the card — not the gift rules.

---

## Roles & succession

There is one **King’s seat** at the table. The person sitting there plays from the King deck; everyone else plays from the Noble deck.

**Succession checks** happen twice each round:
1. After **negotiation**
2. After **playing cards**

At each check, if any Noble has **more earned gold than the current King**, the **highest-earned Noble** (ties broken by Noble play order that round) **takes the crown**.

When someone becomes King:
- They swap **roles, deck, hand, and discard pile** with the former King.
- **Gold stays with the player** — your coins follow you even if you change seats.

Some cards can **block succession** for a check (e.g. *Loyal Guard*).

---

## Round structure

Each game lasts **5 rounds** (or **3** for a short session). Every round:

```
Negotiation → Succession check → Play cards → Succession check → Next round
```

### 1. Negotiation (4 table passes)

Starting from the King and moving in the current turn direction, each player gets **4 full passes** around the table. On your turn you may:

- **Propose a trade** — offer gold and/or cards; the other player may accept or reject.
- **Propose an alliance** — a declared pact between you and one or more players (some cards require an active alliance).
- **Make a conditional promise** — “If X happens, I’ll give you Y.” These are **table talk only** and **not binding** unless a card says otherwise.
- **Threaten or bluff** — social pressure is part of the game.
- **Pass**

**Gift limits** (recommended for balanced play):

| Limit | Amount |
|-------|--------|
| Max gold per single gift | **120** |
| Max gold you can give per negotiation phase | **120** |
| Max gold in one trade (each side) | **120** |
| Max completed trades per player per phase | **2** |

Gold given over these limits is not allowed in tournament-style play; for casual games, use the limits or agree to ignore them.

At the end of negotiation → **succession check**.

### 2. Play cards

Everyone **chooses cards face-down**, then **reveals in order**:

1. **King** plays **3** cards (in the order they chose).
2. Each **Noble** plays **2** cards, in turn order starting from the King’s left (respecting the round’s direction).

Cards resolve one at a time when revealed. Some cards ask you to **pick a target** or **make a choice** (e.g. a risky gamble).

**Redraw** after all cards resolve:
- **King** draws **3** cards.
- Each **Noble** draws **2** cards.

Hands refill up to **8** cards when possible (shuffle your discard into your deck when your deck runs out).

Then → **second succession check** → next round.

## Card timing

| Timing | When it matters |
|--------|-----------------|
| **On reveal** | Resolves when that card is flipped. |
| **Reactive / protection** | Played face-down like any other card; checks whether its condition was true during the round’s play. |
| **End of round** | Resolves after the round’s card play (if any cards use this). |
| **Negotiation only** | Only usable during negotiation (if any cards use this). |

---

## Protection cards (guess right or pay)

**Protection** cards are a gamble:

- Play them **face-down** with your other cards like normal.
- After all cards in the round are revealed, each protection card is checked:
  - **Hit** — you guessed correctly (e.g. someone tried to steal your gold, or a Noble was about to take the crown). You get the card’s benefit.
  - **Whiff** — your read was wrong. You pay the card’s **penalty** (usually losing gold).

The **King resolves first** each round, so King protection against succession is more reliable than Noble protection against attacks that have not happened yet.

---

## Card categories (at a glance)

| Category | What it does |
|----------|----------------|
| **Economy** | Gain, steal, or move gold. |
| **Alliance** | Reward keeping a declared alliance. |
| **Betrayal** | Break an alliance for profit — often needs an active alliance first. |
| **Disruption** | Force discards, skip plays, mess with opponents. |
| **Protection** | Block theft, succession, or attacks — with whiff risk. |
| **Tempo** | Extra draws, extra plays, or skip effects. |
| **Information** | Peek at hidden information. |
| **Supercard** | Rare, high-impact cards with real drawbacks. |

Each player only ever draws from **their role’s deck** — King cards for the King, Noble cards for Nobles.

---

## Winning

When the last round finishes (after the second succession check of round 5, or round 3 in a short game), **whoever sits in the King’s seat wins**.

There is no shared victory — one crowned winner.

## Tips for your first game

1. **Track earned gold** — if you do not split tokens, jot down each player’s earned total when gifts happen.
2. **Negotiation is not optional** — trades and alliances set up betrayal cards and table dynamics.
3. **The King goes first** — when revealing cards, the King’s plays are public before Nobles resolve theirs; plan accordingly.
4. **Gifts are for influence, not the crown** — bribe people to help you win on the board, not to inflate your succession score.
5. **Protection is a read** — do not play defensive cards blindly; they can hurt you if you miss.

---

## Short game variant

For a ~45-minute session:

- Play **3 rounds** instead of 5.
- Keep all other standard numbers (gold, hand size, play/draw counts).
